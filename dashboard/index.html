Syntactica

Every protocol is a grammar.
We learn it, then catch what breaks the rules.

A language model learns the grammar of benign DNS traffic. Anything improbable under that grammar — tunnelling, algorithmically-generated domains, malformed packets — is flagged by its own surprisal, with no attack labels used to train the detector. The same grammar is then turned around to fuzz a resolver.

Detection

Benign-only training, zero-day-ready detection

Both grammars are calibrated on benign traffic at the 99th percentile of surprisal; attack labels are used only to measure. We report threshold-free separability (ROC / PR AUC) and per-family recall — not a single inflated accuracy number.

Conformal operating points (distribution-free FPR guarantee)

Split-conformal calibration on held-out benign traffic sets a threshold whose false-positive rate is provably ≤ α — no Gaussian assumption. Pick your α; the empirical FPR stays under it while recall stays high.

Interpretability

The grammar we extracted

A black-box detector is hard to trust. Syntactica recovers a human-readable stochastic automaton over the shape of domain names — the sequence of label-length buckets. Each cell is the probability of moving from one state (row) to the next (column) in benign traffic. Attacks take transitions this matrix makes rare.

Label-length transition matrix (first-order Markov chain)

Response · Unit IV

From detection to defence: a token-bucket that protects goodput

Detecting is half the job. Syntactica feeds each verdict into a per-source token bucket (the Unit IV traffic-policing algorithm): a query’s token cost scales with its surprisal, so a source flooding high-surprisal exfil queries drains its bucket and is throttled, while ordinary clients sail through. Self-learning

It keeps learning — and stops crying wolf

Real networks drift: a new internal service appears and a frozen detector suddenly flags all of it. The online detector keeps learning from the stream (self-training, with a repetition gate so an attack flood can’t poison it) and absorbs the change. Below, benign traffic shifts at the dashed line.

False-alarm rate over time (benign traffic; lower is better)

When traffic shifts, the static model’s false alarms jump to ~100% and stay there; the self-learning model stays near zero — while both keep catching attacks.

Offense

The grammar fuzzes, too

Sampling from the learned grammar at temperature T reproduces the validity/novelty tradeoff of grammar-guided fuzzing (Learn&Fuzz, ASE’17): low T exercises the parser’s happy path; high T drifts toward the boundary. All traffic is sent to a bundled loopback-only mock resolver.
